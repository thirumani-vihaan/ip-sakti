Agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights (TRIPS) - Articles 27 and 29.
Article 27: patents shall be available for any inventions, whether products or processes, in all fields of technology, provided that they are new, involve an inventive step and are capable of industrial application. Members may exclude from patentability inventions whose commercial exploitation must be prevented to protect ordre public or morality, and may exclude diagnostic, therapeutic and surgical methods, and plants and animals other than micro-organisms. Under Article 27.3(b), plant varieties must be protectable either by patents, by an effective sui generis system, or by a combination of the two.
Article 29: an applicant for a patent must disclose the invention in a manner sufficiently clear and complete for it to be carried out by a person skilled in the art.
Traditional knowledge already in the public domain is generally treated as prior art and is not patentable.
